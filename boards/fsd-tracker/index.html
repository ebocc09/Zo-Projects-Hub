    Measurement

    What the number in each row actually means, so it is not mistaken for something else.

    What counts as using FSD

    A car has to reach this distance on FSD after handoff to count as having used it. Short of that the row stays red, it is left out of the day's drove FSD count, and the host's FSD rate does not credit it — a few tenths of a mile is a bay reposition or a roll off the pad, not a customer choosing the feature.

    Applies to the report already on screen — nothing is measured again, only judged again.
    Post-delivery FSD miles

    GUI_fsdUserTotalMiles at the end of the window, minus the same reading at handoff. Raw lifetime mileage would be misleading — every car arrives with a few FSD miles already banked from lot moves and PDI.

    On a couple of cars a day that counter is stuck: the identical value in every sample for a fortnight, on a car that plainly drove. Subtracting a stuck counter gives a confident zero, which reads as a host who sent someone home without a demo. Those cars are measured on GUI_apFsdTotalMiles instead — the same quantity from another source, which agrees with the usual counter to the thousandth of a mile on every car where both are working. The backup is only ever consulted when the usual one never moves at all, so it can never change a figure the usual one could produce, and a car that genuinely sat still still reads zero. Those rows are marked Backup counter.

    The 12-hour window

    Miles are counted for the first 12 hours after handoff and no longer. That is the drive home and the first evening out — the part of the trip that reflects how the handover went. What the customer does with the car on day three is a different question, and letting it in would mean an older delivery always out-scored a newer one purely for having had more time.

    A row still inside its window is marked Counting and its figure will keep climbing; hover it for the closing time. Once the window has closed the number is final, and the car is never read again — the answer is kept, so re-running a settled date costs nothing and returns exactly what it returned before.

    The baseline

    The last telemetry sample at or before the recorded delivery timestamp. That is close to, but not exactly, the moment of handoff, so any staff driving in between would be counted as customer use. Typical gaps run a few minutes; a row whose gap exceeds an hour is flagged in the list and in the summary.

    Timing

    Today's deliveries are all still counting — a car handed over at 18:00 has barely driven by 19:00, and its window does not close until the early hours. Yesterday's date gives a page of settled numbers.

    Which cars

    Tesladex is asked directly for cars delivered on the date whose vehicle routing location is this centre. The VRL survives delivery — the scheduling block that named the destination does not — and it is indexed, so the whole day arrives in one query.

    This used to work by reading each car's telemetry for the trt_id it held at handoff, which meant probing every car delivered nationally that day: thousands of calls to keep a few dozen, and about twelve minutes. Checked against that method over one full day, the two pick the same cars for this centre exactly — and where they disagree elsewhere, VRL is the better value, naming a centre where telemetry still held a logistics code. That path now runs only for a car with no VRL at all, which so far has never happened.

    The 14-day limit

    Mileage comes from telemetry, which reaches back 336 hours. Past that a car's baseline at handoff has aged out and the delta cannot be measured, so older dates are refused outright rather than quietly returning wrong numbers.

    Sources

    Garage alone in basic mode: tesladex for the population and the delivery timestamp, vitals for the centre and the mileage curve. Advanced adds Intrepid, which is the only place a reference number or a delivery host exists — it is joined on VIN and never used to decide which cars appear.

    Delivery host

    The host is who actually conducted the handover, read from DriverADUserName. That is not the delivery advisor, who owns the appointment — across one centre's deliveries the two differed on roughly a third of cars, so they are not interchangeable.

    Intrepid gives the host as a bare network username with no name beside it. Names are recovered first from the advisor fields on appointments already being read, which arrive as matched username-and-name pairs, and otherwise from a Garage account lookup. A host who cannot be named is shown as their username rather than dropped.

    The two percentages

    Share is that host's slice of the day — their cars over all deliveries. FSD is how many of their own cars were driven on FSD. They answer different questions and are not comparable, so the car count sits beside both: three cars at 100% is not a better day than twenty at 85%.
